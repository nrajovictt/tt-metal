Llama 3.1 8B Prefill on Blackhole

Where one decoder layer spends its kernel time, and how busy the math engines are, for prompt lengths from 128 to 32,768 tokens. The figures here are recomputed on a single, consistent op scope and replace the current Blackhole prefill sheet.

model Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, 1 layer device P150, 1 chip clock 1350 MHz worker grid 110 cores runs 2026-10-13 / 14

Findings

  • 4k → 8k

    SDPA overtakes matmul as the largest share of layer kernel time. At 32k it takes 82%.

  • 73–79%

    FPU utilization inside matmuls, per active core, from 1k up. 66% at 512 and 46% at 128, where the matmuls are too short to fill the cores.

  • ≈ 59%

    SFPU utilization inside SDPA, per active core, from 2k up. 46–47% at 512 and 1k.

  • 3.4×

    Growth in layer kernel time from 16k to 32k (164 → 556 ms), as attention cost goes quadratic.

What changed from the sheet

  1. Op scope switched at 2k. Prefill is traced up to 1k and untraced from 2k on, so the report script picked one decoder layer for 128–1k but the whole forward pass for 2k+. That added the final norm and 8 LM-head matmuls (about 19% of kernel time at 2k) to the larger rows only. MatMul % was inflated by up to 7 pt and SDPA % deflated by up to 5 pt.
  2. FPU definition switched too. For 2k+ the sheet's FPU figure is the median over working cores. That counts only the 8 of 80 LM-head cores that do any work. Every row here uses the median over active cores.
  3. Two rows were padded repeats, and are now re-measured. By default the 512-token prompt is prefilled at 1024 and the 3072-token prompt at 4096, so the sheet's rows for them repeated the 1k and 4k measurements. Both lengths were re-profiled at their real size with TT_PREFILL_MIN_PADDING=1 (a test-only switch, see Method). This moved 512 from 62.05% to 70.81% MatMul and 3072 from 51.86% to 54.24%.

What the whole-layer view shows

  1. The FPU and SFPU swap places as prompts grow. FPU utilization peaks at 53.6% at 1k and falls to 30.0% at 32k, while SFPU climbs from 2.3% to 49.8%. They cross between 8k and 16k. The share of time with at least one unit busy rises from 39% to 80%.
  2. At long prompts, most FPU work is SDPA's own matmuls. At 32k, SDPA supplies 19.6 of the 30.0 FPU points, and QKᵀ and PV make up about 92% of SDPA's FPU cycles. The projection and MLP matmuls supply 10.4 points.
  3. The MLP's SiLU × multiply is the other SFPU user. It keeps the SFPU 58–69% busy and supplies half of all SFPU activity at 512 (3.5 of 7.1 points) and a third at 1k (4.1 of 11.9). From 8k on, SDPA's exp dominates.
  4. Everything else is small. LayerNorm, RoPE, typecasts, the embedding and the residual adds add at most 1.1 FPU points and 0.1 SFPU points. Data-movement ops count as zero and take 0.5–2.9% of layer time (4.6% at 3072).
Ops with no counters
Create heads, concat heads, the two KV-cache writes, and the pad and slice at 3072 launch no compute kernel; the 4k device log shows only data-movement cores for them. They count as 0% on both units, and their time stays in the average.

What this points at

  1. The exponent is the bottleneck. With 256-token chunks one subtract-and-exp step takes 73.4 µs for 64 tiles, about 1.15 µs (1,550 cycles) per tile. The QKᵀ and PV matmuls on the same chunk take 13.1 and 12.3 µs. This matches the perf counters: SFPU busy 59%, FPU 24%.
  2. Two settings are worth an experiment. Prefill SDPA runs with exp_approx_mode=False (model_config.py:1723) and with fp32 destination accumulation (HiFi4). The fp32 setting also keeps SDPA on the older compute loop rather than the streaming one. Both need a PCC check before they could ship.
  3. Short prompts pay per-chunk overhead. Below 2k the chunks are 64 tokens, so the row max and the running-max correction grow to 29–32% of the math thread's time, against 10–11% with 256-token chunks.

Measured with 8 temporary profiling zones in the SDPA compute loop (generated/profiler/sdpa_zones/sdpa_stage_zones.patch, now reverted). Each compute core records only its first ~125 zones, so the mean time of each stage comes from that sample and is multiplied by the exact number of times the stage runs in a causal SDPA. The rebuilt totals cover 96–100% of the measured math-thread time at every length, and the zones did not change SDPA kernel time.

What the split shows

  1. The matmuls run the FPU close to its peak. QKᵀ keeps the FPU busy 93% of its stage time and PV 98%, assuming the FPU's HiFi4 rate of 64 cycles per 32×32×32 tile product. The measured stages take 65–69 cycles per tile product. Matmuls account for 92% of all FPU cycles in SDPA.
  2. 97% of SFPU cycles are the softmax scale and exp. Each subtract-and-exp tile costs about 1,360 SFPU cycles, against about 15 FPU cycles for its subtract. The SFPU is busy 88% of that stage.
  3. The non-approximate path does extra SFPU work per tile. With exp_approx_mode=False the kernel multiplies each tile by 1/√d as a separate SFPU pass, and re-initialises the multiply and the exp for every tile (compute_common.hpp:404–407). Folding the scale into the exp and hoisting the setup out of the loop is a code change to try, separate from switching to approximate exp.
  4. Row max and correction wait more than they compute. Neither unit is busy for 73–76% of those stages; the math thread is waiting on the unpacker or packer. They take about 11% of SDPA time, so this matters less than the exp.

How the split was derived: the unit for each call comes from the kernel code; FPU, SFPU and MATH busy cycles come from the SDPA perf counters (FPU 23.73%, SFPU 59.38%, MATH 83.11% at 32k); stage times come from the profiling zones. Matmul FPU cycles use the HiFi4 rate. The SFPU cost of a subtract-and-exp tile (1,356 cycles) and of the small SFPU ops (142 cycles per tile) are solved from the 64-token and 256-token chunk runs together. The FPU cost of the element-wise tiles (14.7 cycles) is what remains of the FPU counter after the matmuls. Rebuilt from these, the FPU and SFPU cycles per step match the counters to within 0.03%. This split is given for 256-token chunks only. At 64-token chunks the PV stage measures 53 cycles per tile product, faster than the FPU can run it, which means the math thread's timestamps run ahead of the hardware on short stages. In aggregate at 64-token chunks, matmuls are 81% of FPU cycles.

What the layer graph shows

  1. The longest dependency chain is 17.59 ms of the 19.97 ms the layer takes (88%). The chain runs Embeddings → QKV → RoPE Q → SDPA → Wo → W1 → W2. The other 2.38 ms is W3 (2.18 ms, which could run beside W1), the K and V branch (0.14 ms), the KV-cache writes (0.05 ms) and the RoPE table slices (0.01 ms).
  2. Reordering ops has little to give at this length. W1 and W3 each already use 64 of the 110 cores, so running them side by side would not halve their time. Everything else off the chain adds up to 0.2 ms, 1% of the layer.
  3. SDPA does not read the KV cache in prefill. It takes Q, K and V straight from the type-cast tensors. The two cache writes run before SDPA only because the device runs ops in program order; nothing in this layer waits for them.

Inside one SDPA chunk step: data flow

Each core walks one 256-token Q chunk over the K/V chunks, one chunk per step. A step needs the next K and V chunk from DRAM and three running values from the step before: the row max m, the row sum l and the output accumulator O.

Inside one SDPA chunk step: measured schedule

Two consecutive steps on one core (core 1,2) from the profiling zones on the three compute threads. The unpack thread feeds tiles to math, math computes in the destination registers, and pack writes the results back to L1. 1,350 cycles = 1 µs.

QKᵀ matmul Row max Subtract + exp PV matmul Correction

What the SDPA schedule shows

  1. The three threads move through the stages together, and no two stages overlap. Each stage takes the previous stage's output: scores, then max, then exponentials, then P·V, then the corrected output.
  2. Math sets the pace. The unpack thread finishes its subtract-and-exp tiles 7,600 cycles before math does, on every step of every core, and pack follows math within a few hundred cycles.
  3. Data movement keeps up. The next step's QKᵀ starts a median 55 cycles after the correction ends, so the next K and V chunks are already in L1. A step takes a median 149,760 cycles (111 µs) across all 64 cores.
  4. The data dependencies allow more overlap than the kernel uses. QKᵀ for the next chunk needs only Q and the next K chunk, nothing from the current step. It still waits for the current exp, PV and correction for two reasons: one math thread runs the stages in program order, and there is only one scores buffer, which stays in use until PV has read it. Overlapping QKᵀ with the exp would also need the FPU and SFPU to work at the same time, which they never do in this kernel on Blackhole (FPU + SFPU = MATH). The Quasar model below shows what separate threads would allow.

The layer graph's edges come from the model code. The profiler records no tensor IDs, so they are not measured. The SDPA schedule is measured, from the zone run in generated/profiler/sdpa_zones/prefill_4k. The reader and writer threads that move K, V and the output have no zones in these runs, so they are not drawn; the 55-cycle gap between steps is the evidence that they keep up.

What the model shows

  1. An SDPA chunk step drops from 149,918 to 101,318 cycles (1.48×). The SFPU thread is busy the whole time; the FPU thread is busy 48%.
  2. All the FPU work hides behind the exp. While the SFPU computes exp for chunk j, the FPU thread runs PV and the correction for chunk j−1, then QKᵀ and the row-max reduce for chunk j+1. PV for chunk j starts the moment its exp finishes.
  3. After that, the exp alone sets SDPA's speed. Any saving in the exp (approximate mode, folding the 1/√d scale into it, moving its setup out of the tile loop) would then shorten SDPA one for one.
  4. The layer gains most at long prompts: 27% faster at 32k, 12% at 4k, 8% at 2k, because SDPA's share of the layer grows with length.

What the model shows

  1. On one thread, moving work between units cannot make SDPA faster. At the same cost the step stays at 149,911 cycles and only the labels move: SDPA's FPU busy share goes from 23.7% to 21.9% and SFPU from 59.4% to 61.3% (whole layer at 32k: FPU 30.0 → 28.5%, SFPU 49.8 → 51.4%). At the SFPU's own rate the step grows 15%, to 171,669 cycles.
  2. With separate threads and today's exp, it makes things worse: 101,323 → 115,645 cycles. The SFPU thread is already the bottleneck, and now it also carries the row max and the correction.
  3. With separate threads and a single-cycle exp, it balances the threads: 48,588 → 37,060 cycles, 4.05× today against 3.09×. The FPU thread, running only QKᵀ and PV, is busy 88% of the step and the SFPU thread runs back to back. The 32k layer would take 211 ms, against 246 ms with today's split and 556 ms measured.
  4. That gain needs an SFPU as quick as the FPU on element-wise tiles. At 128 cycles per tile instead of about 15, the step is 58,818 cycles, slower than leaving the work on the FPU, and the 32k layer takes 278 ms.
  5. Rule of thumb: move work to the thread that has slack, and only work that the receiving unit does at a similar cost. Today the SFPU has no slack; with a fast exp it does, and then its element-wise speed decides whether the move pays.

What the model shows

  1. With today's exp, the FPU has room during SDPA. The SFPU thread sets the step, and the FPU could run at 40–48% of its clock without lengthening it.
  2. A cheaper exp takes the room away. With a single-cycle exp the FPU thread sets the step and has no headroom; with matmuls only on the FPU the threads are nearly balanced (92%).
  3. Outside SDPA the roles flip. Matmul ops need the FPU at full clock while the SFPU sits idle. A useful policy would switch per phase: slow the FPU during SDPA, or the SFPU during matmuls. SDPA holds 65% of the layer's FPU-busy cycles at 32k but only 11% at 2k.
  4. A lower frequency on its own saves little. Energy per FPU operation scales with C·V². At the same voltage the FPU does the same work for the same energy, slow or fast, and SDPA takes as long as before, so leakage is unchanged. A lower clock removes only the clock-distribution energy and the toggling of idle cycles, which clock gating may already remove.
  5. Real savings need the voltage to drop with the frequency, on a power domain that holds the FPU. For example, a 20% lower voltage at half clock would cut the FPU's dynamic energy during SDPA by about 36% (0.8² = 0.64). The actual figure depends on Quasar's voltage–frequency curve.
Method
The FPU-thread tasks of the Quasar model are stretched by a factor until the step grows by more than 0.1%; the lowest clock is 1 / that factor.
What the code shows
Quasar has separate clock domains for the data-movement cores and the Neo compute engines, both 1.4 GHz in the profiler code (profiler_analysis.cpp). Nothing found puts the FPU and SFPU in separate domains; both are in the Neo engine. Blackhole has one chip-wide clock with only a firmware busy/idle switch (200 ms timeout), while ops last from microseconds to tens of milliseconds.
Needed for watts
Whether the FPU and SFPU have their own clock and voltage domains, Quasar's voltage–frequency curve, how fast a domain can switch, whether idle FPU cycles are clock-gated, and the FPU's share of chip power.

Method

Op scope
Embedding plus one decoder layer: 21 ops, from Embeddings to the MLP residual add. For 128 and 1k this is exactly the replayed prefill trace. For every other length it is the same ops picked out of the last eager forward pass, leaving out the final norm, the LM head (8 × 32×4096×16032 matmuls) and sampling.
Prompt length
Prefill normally rounds the prompt up to 128, 1024 or the next power of 2. The 512 and 3072 runs set TT_PREFILL_MIN_PADDING=1, which makes test_model_prefill.py round up only as far as the model needs (multiples of 128, 512 or 1024). Model and serving code are unchanged, and those runs are untraced, which affects host overhead but not device kernel time.
Matmul / SDPA
Op codes matching matmul (Matmul, MinimalMatmul) and sdpa. Shares are of total device kernel time in scope.
Active core
For each op, take the median over the cores that ran it of counter ÷ that core's counter window. Then average over ops, weighted by kernel time.
Full grid
For each op, take counter ÷ op kernel duration, averaged over all 110 worker cores with idle cores counted as 0. This is the chip-level number.
Counters
FPU and SFPU count the cycles in which an instruction is valid on that unit; MATH counts cycles in which either is. All three are read per core over the op's kernel window.

Reproduce

# 128, 1k: prefill is traced, and the default scope is the trace replay
python tools/tracy/perf_util_report.py generated/profiler/prefill_1k_1dev

# 2k, 4k and up: prefill is eager, so give the layer's op rows explicitly
python tools/tracy/perf_util_report.py generated/profiler/prefill_32k_1dev --ops-range 1113:1135

# 512 and 3072 at their real length (capture, then analyse)
TT_PREFILL_MIN_PADDING=1 MAX_PREFILL_CHUNK_SIZE=32 python -m tracy -r -p -v \
    --profiler-capture-perf-counters=fpu --op-support-count=100000 -o generated/profiler/prefill_3k_1dev_nopad \
    -m "pytest models/tt_transformers/tests/test_model_prefill.py -k '1layer and not accuracy and max128k-3k-'"
python tools/tracy/perf_util_report.py generated/profiler/prefill_3k_1dev_nopad --ops-range 658:682   # 512: 658:680

# read: SUMMARY → "Matmul % of total", "SDPA % of total",
#       FPU / SFPU blocks → tw-avg of "median per ACTIVE core" and "mean per FULL-GRID core"

The --ops-range form also picks up two small rotary-table Slice ops, so its shares come out within 0.04 pt of the table above.

Data checks

  • Every run passed its PCC checks (output PCC 0.998 at every length).
  • No run log contains the profiler's "DRAM buffers were full, markers were dropped" warning.
  • Counter-derived per-op utilization matches the profiler's own ops CSV columns to within 10⁻¹⁴ pt, and raw kernel windows match the reported kernel durations to within 0.04%.
  • Every in-scope op has device timing, and every matmul and SDPA op has perf-counter data.
  • The nine SDPA stage-zone runs (generated/profiler/sdpa_zones, 2026-10-14) also passed PCC and dropped no profiler markers.
